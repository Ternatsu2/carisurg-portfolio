Accessibility considerations

Nurse-facing emergency-triage queue and alert panel | fictional shadow-mode prototype

A tired clinician must be able to identify the required action even when colour, sound, screen quality, or ideal viewing conditions are unavailable.

Meaning beyond colour

  • Words, tier label, symbol, border and queue position repeat urgency.
  • Plain-text ranking is tested before colour is introduced.
  • Greyscale or colour-vision difference must not reverse the order.

Readable under pressure

  • Primary alerts use active, plain-English wording in twelve words or fewer.
  • The action comes before supporting data and stays within two lines.
  • Body text begins at 16 px with AA contrast and generous line spacing.
  • Abbreviations remain outside the primary alert.

Keyboard, zoom and motion

  • Visible order and keyboard order match.
  • Every control needs a clear label and visible focus state.
  • At 200% zoom, tier, action and primary control remain in one reading path.
  • No flashing content or essential animation is used.

Hearing and workload

  • Sound may reinforce a validated alert but never carries it alone.
  • Medium and low states are silent by default to limit alarm fatigue.
  • Acknowledge or mute keeps the visible state and audit trail.
  • One primary action is shown at a time; manual triage remains available.

Known limits and planned checks

This initial prototype has not been clinically validated and has not yet been tested with clinicians or assistive-technology users. Peer testing will cover words-only ranking, greyscale, 200% zoom, keyboard order, glare simulation, alert recognition, error recovery, and confirm/override. Clinical timing, sound patterns, local terminology, screen-reader behaviour, and use under real emergency-department workload require later specialist review.

Draft co-design canvas

Emergency triage HCI | draft for nurse and Clinical IT review

Design question How might a triage screen show a model's suggested ESI level without slowing the encounter or replacing the nurse's judgement?

Setting

Where: Mercer General ED triage workstation.

When: After complaint and vital signs are recorded, before the nurse finalises ESI.

Context: Noise, interruptions, queue pressure, night shifts, and unstable network or power.

Users

Primary: ED triage nurse, who owns the final ESI decision.

Secondary: Charge nurse, ED clinician, Clinical IT, governance and safety reviewers.

One active nurse per case

Task and system mode

  1. Review imported intake values.
  2. Request a model suggestion.
  3. Confirm or override it.
  4. Record the nurse's final ESI.
Nurse-controlled HCI

Inputs

Selected route: EHR pull with manual correction and fallback.

Age, chief complaint, arrival mode, pulse, BP, respiratory rate, SpO2, temperature, glucose, and oxygen context.

Show source, collection time, and validation state for every value.

Output and display

Suggested ESI 1 to 5 with a number, urgency label, and redundant colour cue.

Keep the reviewed complaint and vital signs beside the suggestion.

Do not invent a confidence score or causal explanation.

Human action

Confirm: nurse accepts the suggested ESI.

Override: nurse selects an ESI and records clinical judgement, new information, a data-quality issue, or another reason.

Keep the model value, human value, user, reason and time in the audit trail.

Failure, mitigation and residual risk

Under-triage: keep the nurse as decision owner; the suggestion may still anchor judgement.

Alert fatigue: use one high-acuity reminder; the alert may still be missed.

Missing or stale data: show source and time, block the model request, and allow manual correction.

Service outage: show an unavailable state and return to manual triage without losing entered values.

Interface clutter: keep data, suggestion and decision controls in one reading path.

Accessibility: pair colour with text and number; test contrast, glare and language needs with users.

Co-design checks

  • Which values must stay visible?
  • When should the suggestion appear?
  • Which override reasons fit existing practice?
  • Is a 60-second reminder useful or distracting?
  • Who can reverse a decision, and when?

Review with: triage and charge nurses, ED clinician, Clinical IT, accessibility/patient experience, and governance.

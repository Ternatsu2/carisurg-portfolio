Stationary intake assistant | patient choice and nurse handoff

Design question How might a stationary embodied assistant help patients communicate why they came to the ED while protecting dignity, preventing contamination, and making nurse handoff immediate?

Setting

Where: Marked intake position visible to the triage desk but screened from the waiting area.

When: Optional pre-triage complaint capture for walk-in patients.

Context: Noise, mixed languages, limited privacy, mobility needs, infection controls, and power or network loss.

Users and need

Primary: patient who chooses the assistant and the nurse who reviews the draft.

Secondary: caregiver, interpreter, infection prevention, biomedical engineering, Clinical IT, accessibility and governance.

Patient may decline

Form and boundary

Stationary 120 cm unit, locked base, wipeable shell, adjustable screen, microphone, speaker, physical nurse-call and emergency-stop controls.

No human face, moving arms, autonomous navigation, diagnosis, ESI display, advice or treatment.

Interaction

  1. State purpose and obtain consent.
  2. Choose language and touch, push-to-talk or nurse mode.
  3. Ask one short question at a time.
  4. Read back and display each answer.
  5. Review, correct, erase or send the draft.
  6. Hand off to the nurse queue.

Behaviour and output

Neutral, concise prompts with captions and a visible progress marker.

"I recorded that" rather than simulated empathy. If audio is unclear, ask again or call a nurse instead of guessing.

Output is an unverified complaint draft, access preferences, consent state, times and device-health state.

Human action

Patient: continue, correct, erase, decline or call a nurse at any point.

Nurse: review the draft, assess the patient, and decide whether any information is clinically useful.

The nurse, not the assistant or model, decides final ESI.

Failure, mitigation and residual risk

Excess trust: repeat "assistant - nurse decides"; embodiment may still imply authority.

Infection: voice-first path, cleaning status and lockout; a false cleaning log remains possible.

Noise and speech error: push-to-talk, captions and read-back; a patient may still confirm an error.

Privacy: directional audio, privacy position and erase-before-send; speech can still be overheard.

Physical safety: locked base, clear zone, rounded edges and stop switch; the unit can still obstruct movement.

Outage or degradation: health check, low-battery warning and nurse route; failure can still cause frustration.

Co-design checks

  • Do patients understand that this is not a clinician?
  • Can every user decline or call a nurse unaided?
  • Are speech, captions and touch usable in ED noise?
  • Is cleaning status credible and easy to audit?
  • Does placement protect privacy and clear evacuation paths?

Test with: patients, caregivers, nurses, infection prevention, biomedical engineering and accessibility reviewers.

Completed design baseline for usability and physical-safety testing. No working robot or clinical validation. Terry Benjamin Jr. | 4 August 2026
